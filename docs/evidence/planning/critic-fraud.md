## 0. Conclusions (one page)

1. **The plan's fraud model is "server recomputes everything it can, flags the rest" (lens-security §7.1), and that is the right model.** Geo verdict, prices, totals, points, business date and scope are all recomputed server-side (lens-security §6.4, §5.4; lens-data DQ-13/14/23/24/26). What the server cannot recompute — the raw GPS fix, quantities, the paid amount, the time of capture, the photo — is exactly where every surviving attack lives.
2. **Casual fake GPS is handled; serious fake GPS is not.** `isMocked` per fix, integrity signals and the plausibility rules (lens-security §2.4, FS-08; lens-data DQ-23/25) catch Play-Store mock apps. A rooted phone with Magisk DenyList and an LSPosed hook that clears the mock flag, a "walking" joystick that adds realistic jitter, or an SDR GNSS simulator produce fixes the plan accepts. The one cheap, battery-free signal nobody collects is the **radio environment** (serving cell IDs, visible Wi-Fi BSSIDs) at fix time; a GPS position 8 km from the cell that served the request is the tell. G-fraud-01.
3. **The two lenses disagree on how to defeat clock back-dating, and one of them loses.** lens-sync stamps rows with a `clock_offset_ms` measured at the last server contact (§3.4, D-sync-13); lens-security anchors to `elapsedRealtime` + `boot_id` (§5.4, D-sec-09). An SR who changes the clock *after* the last sync defeats the offset method completely; only the monotonic anchor survives. The merge must adopt D-sec-09 as primary. A second hole: `cfg.sync.max_backdate_days` = 7 lets month-end sales be stamped into a closed month when the anchor is lost (reboot). G-fraud-02, the one blocker here.
4. **The local store is not tamper-evident.** lens-sync chooses no SQLCipher (D-sync-01); lens-security chooses SQLCipher (§9); on a rooted phone neither matters because the key is in the process. The server trusts `lat/lng`, `qty`, `paid_mtk`, `captured_at` as sent. Signing each record at capture with the Keystore device key the plan already creates for `X-Device-Proof` (lens-security §2.1) makes every "edit SQLite / craft a batch with curl" attack require live code injection into the signed app, and Keystore attestation at bind (`attestationApplicationId`) tells the server whether the app that holds the key is AKTCL's build or a repack. G-fraud-03/04.
5. **Money fraud is invisible without a counter-party.** Fabricated cash memos, skimmed due collections and under-reported stock issue all balance on the SR's own numbers (FS-17 compares SR-reported issue with SR-reported sales). The field critic's G-field-01 (distribution house as an actor; `cash_handover` and confirmed `stock_movement`) is, from this chair, the single most valuable anti-fraud control in the whole plan and should be treated as a fraud control, not only a workflow gap. G-fraud-07.
6. **The approval chain can be walked by the people who hold two keys.** A TSO can reset an SR's password *and* issue the OTP that binds a new phone to that SR, then sell as the SR for a whole territory (lens-security §2.2 "Admin reset", §2.3). An AMO can verify a ghost outlet or a 2 km location move from the office. A `cfg.field` holder can raise every fraud threshold to 100 and the catalogue goes dark, at risk class C1 with no second person. G-fraud-06/08/09/10.
7. **Config safety rails look one way.** The anomaly watch fires on a *drop* in geo-valid % (lens-config §4.9, `cfg.sla.geo_valid_drop_alert_pts`); a TSO who loosens a radius to 2,000 m inside the bounds makes geo-valid % *rise*. Break-glass can apply any value, including a loosening. Master data (prices, targets, holidays, visit-day exceptions) has no "future-dated only" rule, so history can be rewritten through the front door. G-fraud-09/11/21.
8. **Three ordinary web-app vulnerabilities are not in any lens**: spreadsheet formula injection through free-text fields into the Excel exports (an outlet named `=HYPERLINK(...)`), ESC/POS control bytes and Unicode direction overrides in names that reach the printer and the approval panel, and a wave-day lockout denial-of-service against 8,500 predictable usernames (`sr334001`…). G-fraud-12/14.
9. **PII leaves in rows, not in dumps.** The export path is well guarded (`pii` claim, re-auth, export log). The paginated `GET /outlets` with phone columns at 60 requests/min and no row budget is not: a WM persona can page 70k outlets in an hour. G-fraud-13.
10. **Supply chain is strong on the API, weaker on the APK.** The in-app updater verifies sha256 against an `app_release` row written by the same CI that built the APK; a compromised runner signs what it injected. Android's signature check is the real backstop, which makes the APK signing key and an independent rebuild the two things to protect. G-fraud-16.

Count: 92 attacks analysed (A-01..A-92); 30 **P** (two of them P for code and D for content), 23 **D**, 22 **PD**, 17 **G**. 26 gaps (1 blocker, 17 major, 8 minor). 10 new signals (FS-20..FS-29), 9 new DQ rules (DQ-35..DQ-43), 27 config-key rows (≈ 40 keys, §4.4), 16 test gates (`T-x-1n`).
